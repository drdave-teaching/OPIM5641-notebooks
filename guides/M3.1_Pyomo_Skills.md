## 🧰 The Pyomo framework — the five things
*Notebooks: `0_General_Framework_LP_Pyomo_blank.ipynb` · `_answers.ipynb`*

- [ ] Recite the five steps cold: **import → `ConcreteModel()` → `Var` → `Objective` → `Constraint`**
- [ ] Explain what `ConcreteModel()` *is* — a blank workspace you append attributes to
- [ ] Declare a decision variable with `domain=NonNegativeReals`, and say why the domain is the part that matters
- [ ] Use `bounds=(lo, hi)` as an alternative to writing demand ceilings as separate constraints
- [ ] Write an `Objective` with an expression and `sense=maximize` (or `minimize`)
- [ ] Write constraints two ways: the engineering way (`Constraint1`, `Constraint2`…) and the descriptive way (`model.fabrication`)
- [ ] Use `pprint()` to audit your model before solving — and know it's the actual file handed to the solver
- [ ] Count your declarations (**3 variables + 1 objective + 6 constraints = 10 things**)
- [ ] Solve with `SolverFactory('cbc', executable=...)` and find **Termination condition: optimal**
- [ ] Print results correctly — **`model.profit()` with parentheses**, not `model.profit`
- [ ] Get **\$8,400 at (0, 275, 100)** and recognize it as the same answer from brute force, graphical and Simplex

## 📊 Allocation models
*Notebook: `1_Allocation_Models.ipynb`*

- [ ] Recognize an allocation model on sight: **limited resource, competing uses → maximize, all $\le$**
- [ ] Print the left-hand side of every constraint after solving
- [ ] Identify which constraints are **binding** (LHS = RHS) and which have **slack**
- [ ] Say why binding constraints matter: **they're the bottleneck jamming up the business**

## 💰 Shadow prices and sensitivity analysis
*Notebook: `1_Allocation_Models.ipynb` (sensitivity section at the bottom)*

- [ ] Define a **shadow price**: the profit gained from one more unit of a binding resource
- [ ] Compare a shadow price against the **real cost** of the resource to make a business call (\$4 of profit vs. \$1 to run the machine → take it)
- [ ] Build the sweep yourself: **put the model in a for loop** and vary the RHS of the binding constraint
- [ ] Buffer the range on **both sides** of the current value so you can see what happens before and after
- [ ] Collect results into a DataFrame and read the **product mix shifting** as capacity grows
- [ ] Compute the shadow price empirically: **`.diff()` on profit, divided by the step size**
- [ ] Explain **diminishing returns** — why the shadow price decays toward zero (*diamonds versus sand*)
- [ ] Find the **stopping point** and turn it into a recommendation you could take to a manager
- [ ] Explain why plotting **total profit alone is misleading**, and what to plot instead

## 🥜 Covering models
*Notebook: `2_CoveringModels.ipynb`*

- [ ] Recognize a covering model: **meet every minimum standard at least cost → minimize, all $\ge$**
- [ ] Set it up in Pyomo with five decision variables and four nutritional constraints
- [ ] Say which solution methods apply — and **why the graphical method can't** (more than two variables)
- [ ] Read an optimal answer that is **mathematically perfect and commercially absurd** (\$7.53 of coconut flakes)
- [ ] Fix it by adding **minimum-quantity constraints**, and know you could equally have used `bounds`
- [ ] Explain what the extra realism **cost** you (\$7.53 → \$8.33) — the price of the promise you're making

## ☕ Blending models
*Notebook: `3_BlendingModels_Coffee.ipynb`*

- [ ] Recognize a blending model: **hit a weighted-average specification**
- [ ] Write a weighted average as a fraction, and sanity-check it by setting all variables to 1
- [ ] Explain why a fraction **can't** be used directly as a constraint (and that solvers dislike them)
- [ ] Clear the fraction: multiply both sides through, **collect terms**, land on $-3B - 18C + 7P \ge 0$
- [ ] Recognize the cleared form as the *same* constraint, just scaled
- [ ] Identify which blending constraint is **binding** and explain the recipe from it — cheap-and-aromatic beans get used up, expensive ones close the gap, weak ones get sprinkled in
