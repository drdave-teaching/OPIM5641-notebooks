## 🔗 Linking constraints — coupling yes/no to how-much
*Notebook: `8_Integer/Fixed_VariableCosts_Mayhugh.ipynb`*

- [ ] Name what breaks the plain LP: **fixed costs** — a sales team costs the same at one unit or a million
- [ ] Build the lazy model first (subtract all fixed costs as a constant) and get **$410** — then say what it *can't* ask
- [ ] Write the product-maximum linking constraint **$x \le M\,y$**, rearranged to $x - M y \le 0$
- [ ] Say both cases out loud: **y = 0 forces x = 0 and skips the fixed cost; y = 1 pays it and buys back the ceiling**
- [ ] Identify **M** in this problem: the demand ceiling, doing double duty as the big-M
- [ ] Move each fixed cost into the objective attached to its y: $1.2x_1 - 60y_1 + 1.8x_2 - 200y_2 + 2.2x_3 - 100y_3$
- [ ] Read the solver's verdict like a consultant: **drop F3 entirely, profit $410 → $508** — the model found "you make more by not doing something"
- [ ] Sit with the sobering part: *"those are people in the sales department, and your model said, let them go"*
